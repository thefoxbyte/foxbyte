Thank you for contributing to FoxByte. This agreement sets out what you allow us
to do with the code you contribute. Its structure follows the Apache Software
Foundation's Individual Contributor License Agreement, with changes made for
Indian law, which this agreement is governed by.

Please read it. The short version is below, but the short version is not the
agreement — clauses 1 to 11 are.

## In short

- **You keep your copyright.** Your contribution stays yours. You can use it
  anywhere else, for anything, forever. This is a licence to us, not a transfer
  of ownership.
- **You keep your moral rights.** Indian law gives you the right to be identified
  as the author and to object to treatment of your work that harms your
  reputation. Those cannot be signed away, and this agreement does not try.
- **You give us a broad licence**, worldwide and for the full life of the
  copyright, including the right to release your contribution under different
  terms later.
- **Those terms may be commercial.** FoxByte has a paid edition under a
  commercial licence. Your contribution may end up there. We would rather say so
  plainly than have you discover it afterwards.
- **You confirm you may give it** — that you wrote it, that you are old enough to
  enter a contract, and that if you wrote it on an employer's time or equipment,
  they are content for you to contribute it.

If you are not comfortable with any of that, please tell us before you open a
pull request rather than after. We would rather lose a contribution than take one
on terms its author did not want.

## How to sign

Open a pull request. A bot will comment with a link, and signing takes one click.
You only ever do this once, no matter how many contributions follow.

If that is not working, you can sign in the pull request itself by leaving a
comment with exactly this line:

```
I have read the FoxByte CLA and I hereby sign it.
```

Either method is an electronic record of Your acceptance. Under section 10A of
the Information Technology Act, 2000, a contract is not unenforceable merely
because it was formed electronically.

### Contributing on behalf of a company

If your employer owns the rights to your work, we need a **Corporate** CLA signed
by someone able to bind the company, rather than this individual one. Please get
in touch before opening a pull request and we will sort it out.

## Why we ask for this

FoxByte's core is open source under AGPL-3.0-or-later, and there is a paid
edition under a commercial licence. Keeping both means we need to hold the rights
to everything in the tree.

Without a CLA, each contributor keeps the copyright to their own code, and we
would need to track down every past contributor individually before making a
licensing decision — people change jobs and email addresses, and some simply
never reply. One signature at the start avoids a search later that may not
succeed.

Your own rights are untouched by this. **It is a licence to us, not a transfer of
ownership** — deliberately. Some projects ask contributors to assign copyright
outright, which hands over the work entirely. We ask for a licence instead,
because it gives us what we actually need to keep an open core and a paid edition
side by side, and leaves your work yours.

## Notes for the reviewing lawyer

*This section is not part of the agreement. It records why the Apache original
was changed, and the questions we could not answer ourselves. Delete it once the
agreement is settled.*

The Apache ICLA is drafted for US law. Four features of Indian law drove the
changes:

1. **Consideration — section 25, Indian Contract Act, 1872.** An agreement made
   without consideration is void, and none of the three statutory exceptions fits
   a CLA. The Apache original recites no consideration, which may be fine in the
   US and looked unsafe here, so clause 2 was added. **Is the consideration
   recited there sufficient, or should there be nominal monetary consideration
   instead?**

2. **Licences take the assignment rules — section 30A, Copyright Act, 1957.**
   Sections 19 and 19A apply to a licence under section 30 with necessary
   adaptations. That pulls in defaults that would otherwise gut this agreement:
   - **Section 19(5)** — if the period is not stated, it is deemed **five years**.
   - **Section 19(6)** — if the territorial extent is not specified, it is
     presumed to extend **within India only**.
   - **Section 19(2)** — the instrument is to identify the work and specify the
     rights, duration and territorial extent; **section 19(3)** concerns the
     royalty payable.

   Clause 3 therefore states duration, territory and royalty as separate express
   terms rather than relying on the words "perpetual, worldwide, royalty-free"
   alone, and clause 1 says how a Contribution is identified. **Please confirm
   the wording is specific enough to displace each default.**

3. **Lapse for non-exercise — section 19(4).** Where the assignee does not
   exercise the rights within one year, the assignment is "deemed to have
   lapsed". Applied to a licence, a contribution we accepted but did not ship for
   a year could see its licence lapse. Clause 3 states that the licence does not
   lapse for non-exercise. **Can section 19(4) be contracted out of, and if not,
   what is the right mitigation?** This is the question we are least sure about.

4. **Moral rights — section 57.** These are not waivable in India and survive
   assignment. Rather than include a waiver that would not hold, clause 5
   acknowledges that they remain with the author. **Is acknowledging them the
   right approach, or should the clause be silent?**

Also outstanding:

- **Writing and signature — sections 19(1) and 30** require a licence to be in
  writing and signed by the owner or a duly authorised agent. We rely on section
  10A of the Information Technology Act, 2000 and on an electronic record of
  acceptance (a click or a comment). **Is that a sufficient "signature" for these
  sections, or is an electronic signature under the IT Act needed?**
- **Stamp duty.** Whether this agreement attracts stamp duty in the relevant
  State, and if so how an electronically accepted agreement should be stamped.
  Stamp duty is a State subject, so this follows from where the agreement is
  treated as made.
- **Patent licence formalities** under the Patents Act, 1970, including whether
  anything must take a particular form or be registered.
- **Future contributions.** The agreement covers present and future
  Contributions. **Does that hold for works not yet in existence, and does
  anything need to be said about when the licence attaches to each?**
- **The Corporate CLA**, which does not exist yet and is currently handled by
  asking contributors to get in touch.
- **The forum.** Clause 11 keeps disputes in India without naming a seat, leaving
  the court to be worked out under the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908. Naming a
  specific city would narrow it further and make the venue predictable. **Is
  naming one worth doing, and does leaving it open create any difficulty in
  enforcing against a contributor outside India?**
